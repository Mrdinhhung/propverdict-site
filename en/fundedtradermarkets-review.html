Funded Trader Markets (FTM) Review: 1-Step, 2-Step, Instant & Profit Split (2026)

Updated: Nov 28, 2026 · Data source: fundedtradermarkets.com

Funded Trader Markets (FTM) has a fairly complex legal structure spanning three jurisdictions, per its footer disclosure: FTM Funded Trader Markets LTD (Cyprus, registration HE462185, 56 Alekou Konstantinou, Strovolos, Nicosia) acts as a "payment agent" receiving payments on behalf of the main entity; Formed Technologies INT FZCO (UAE, registration 36580, Dubai Digital Park, Dubai Silicon Oasis) is the "contracting entity" for all clients and holds the cTrader/TradeLocker platform arrangements; Funded Trader Markets LTD (Saint Lucia, registration 2025-00239) holds the MetaTrader 5 licence. The site states plainly: "The contracting entity for all clients is Formed Technologies INT FZCO (UAE)." Service is unavailable to residents of Cuba, Syria, Iran, Lebanon, Iraq, Yemen, North Korea, Russia — and, notably, Cyprus itself, even though one entity in the group is incorporated there.

Important note: FTM states: "FTM provides simulated trading accounts within a non-live environment, created solely to support education and skill development. We do not offer investment or financial advice." All trading is simulated; no real capital or investment service is involved.

Trading-freedom policies (as published by FTM)

FTM has a dedicated section called "Trade with Freedom at FTM," stating it does not apply several behavioral rules common at other prop firms:

  • No gambling/toxic-trading ban: FTM considers the concept too subjective to apply as a rule; risk is controlled via drawdown limits instead.
  • No lot-size restriction: any lot size your available margin permits is allowed, with no penalty for inconsistent sizing between trades.
  • Martingale/layering allowed: on a single account; running multiple accounts simultaneously to create an unfair advantage is treated as abusive and leads to reward deductions.
  • No news restrictions: no blackout windows, no reward reduction tied to trade timing around news events.
  • No margin usage rule: leverage is fixed at a known level; using all available margin is not treated as a violation.
  • No one-sided betting ban: traders may open additional same-direction trades on a symbol while an existing position (in profit or loss) is open, with no mandatory cooldown.

Platforms & entity-to-platform mapping

Per the entity disclosures, FTM offers three platforms: MetaTrader 5 (MT5) (via Funded Trader Markets LTD's Saint Lucia licence), cTrader and TradeLocker (via Formed Technologies INT FZCO, UAE). cTrader/MT5 services are not intended for U.S. residents or jurisdictions where their use would violate local law.

Payouts & self-reported figures

The homepage displays a "$11.51M PAID & COUNTING" counter alongside a "Recent Settlements" feed showing many real-looking reward amounts (mostly in the $100–$5,000 range, with some larger, e.g. $8,770). The "Countries Covered," "Max Allocation" and "Reward Split" stat tiles render as animated counters — PropVerdict could not extract their final static values in this check, only the general marketing line "Up to 90% Profit Split" and "MetaTrader 5 Available." The FAQ has a dedicated "Payments and Withdrawals" topic describing a "24-hour payout guarantee," but PropVerdict did not extract full detail (minimum amount, specific methods) in this pass.

An objective look

FTM operates through three entities across three jurisdictions (Cyprus, UAE, Saint Lucia) with clearly separated roles (payment agent / contracting entity / platform licence holder) — a disclosure that's detailed but fairly complex for an ordinary trader to fully track. Excluding Cyprus itself from the list of served countries, while one group entity is incorporated there, is a detail PropVerdict noted without finding further explanation on-site. The "trading freedom" policy (no news, lot-size or martingale restrictions) is a genuine positive differentiator versus many firms that ban these techniques — but it also means capital protection relies entirely on the drawdown limits, so traders need to self-manage risk more carefully. The parallel existence of "1-Step Nitro" (in the comparison table) and "1-Step Infinity/Infinity Plus" (in the detailed FAQ), with different profit-split figures (90% upfront vs. an 80%→85%→90% escalator), is an unresolved point traders should confirm directly with FTM before buying, to be sure which product they're actually purchasing.

PropVerdict's take

Funded Trader Markets stands out for a relatively permissive trading policy (no news/lot-size restrictions, martingale allowed per account) and a varied product range from 1-Step to Instant Funding with low entry fees (from $20-25). That said, the cross-border three-entity structure and the naming inconsistency between the main comparison table and the detailed FAQ are points traders should clarify with support before paying, particularly around the escalating profit-split program (Infinity), which appears to apply to a specific variant not clearly confirmed in the main comparison table.
