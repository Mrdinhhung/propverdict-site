What Is Prop Trading? How It Works & Risks to Know

Updated: 2026-11-19 · PropVerdict

Prop trading (short for "proprietary trading") is a model where a firm — a prop trading firm — provides a trader with a trading account, instead of the trader risking their own capital directly on the market. If the trader trades profitably and meets the firm's conditions, they receive a share of that profit.

How the common model works

Most retail prop firms today follow this structure:

  • Buy a "challenge" or evaluation: the trader pays a fee to attempt a challenge with a specific profit target and drawdown limit over a set period.
  • Pass the evaluation: if the profit target is reached without breaching the drawdown rules, the trader receives a "funded" account.
  • Trade on a simulated account: the most important thing to understand — most "funded" accounts at retail prop firms today are simulated (demo) accounts, not real capital traded directly on the market. Simulated profits are paid out to the trader in real money once payout conditions are met.
  • Profit split & payouts: each firm has its own profit split (commonly 70%–90% to the trader) and its own payout cycle.

Benefits of prop trading

  • No need for large upfront capital — only the challenge fee, which is much lower than the account size granted.
  • Clearly limited downside (you only risk the challenge fee if you fail, not a large capital loss).
  • A chance to validate trading skill and earn income if you trade consistently profitably.

Risks & what to know

  • It is not real capital: as noted above, trading happens on a simulated environment at most retail prop firms — traders should understand this clearly rather than assume they are given real capital to trade on the live market.
  • Pass rates and first-payout rates are not high: independent reviews consistently show that most challenge buyers do not pass, or do not reach their first payout.
  • Repeat costs: breaching drawdown rules usually means buying a new challenge — costs can add up over multiple attempts.
  • Quality varies a lot between firms: payout speed, transparency around profit split, and real-world reputation differ significantly — check independent reviews before choosing a firm.

How to choose the right firm

Before signing up with any prop firm, compare: challenge fees relative to account size, profit target and max drawdown, profit split, minimum withdrawal and payout processing time, and how transparent the terms are. PropVerdict breaks down these criteria in each individual firm review.
