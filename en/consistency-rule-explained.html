What Is a Consistency Rule in Prop Trading?

Updated: 2026-11-22 · PropVerdict

A consistency rule is a term some prop trading firms use to cap how much of a payout period's total profit can come from a single trading day or a single position. The goal is to stop a trader from getting lucky on one oversized trade and cashing out immediately, instead of demonstrating stable, repeatable trading skill over time.

Why prop firms use a consistency rule

Without this rule, a trader could take one extremely high-risk trade, hit a huge win on a single day, then withdraw the entire profit right away — behavior that looks more like a one-off gamble than the sustainable trading style a prop firm actually wants to reward. A consistency rule forces profit to be spread across multiple days or multiple trades, which better reflects a trader's real long-term risk management.

Common forms of a consistency rule

  • Maximum % of profit from a single day: for example, no single day can account for more than 40%-50% of the total profit for the payout period. If one day exceeds the cap, the excess amount may be excluded from the withdrawable total.
  • Maximum % of profit from a single position/trade: more common at firms offering futures trading, typically capping a single trade at around 30%-40% of total profit.
  • Minimum number of profitable trading days: a related form — some programs (for example High Stakes and Pro Growth at The5ers, per PropVerdict's The5ers review) require at least 3 profitable days before a pass counts, to prevent hitting the target through a single trade.

What happens if you breach it?

Unlike a drawdown breach (which usually closes the account immediately), breaching a consistency rule at most firms only affects the amount you can withdraw: the payout request may be held, reduced, or the trader may be asked to add more smaller profitable days to dilute the weight of the single largest day before the full payout is processed. The funded account itself usually isn't closed for this reason alone — but exact handling varies by firm.

How to check before signing up

Not every prop firm clearly states a consistency rule on its main terms page, and the threshold can differ between programs at the same firm (for example, only applying at the funded stage, not during evaluation). PropVerdict notes: InstantFunding Lite explicitly states it has no consistency rule (see PropVerdict's InstantFunding review), while information on The5ers' consistency rule is not consistent across independent reviews (see PropVerdict's The5ers review) — traders should always read the current FAQ/Terms of the exact program they plan to buy, not rely on third-party reviews alone.
