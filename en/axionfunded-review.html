AxionFunded Review: 1-Step, 2-Step, Instant, Drawdown & Profit Split (2026)

Updated: Nov 26, 2026 · Data source: axionfunded.com

AxionFunded is a prop trading brand operated by Rocksby Ltd — per the Terms: "Axion Funded is a brand and service operated by Rocksby Ltd, its parent operating company." The homepage does not state a founding year (only a "© 2026 Rocksby Ltd" copyright line), does not list a registered office address, and does not mention any financial regulatory registration. The "Axion Network" section lists London, Dubai, Singapore, New York, Sydney and Hong Kong, but presents these as a trader community, not company offices.

Important note: AxionFunded states: "Axion is not a broker, does not accept investment deposits and does not provide financial advice" and "Simulated trading only · No client funds · No investment services." All balances, activity and profit shown are simulated; a "reward" is described by AxionFunded as "a contractual payment made by Axion from its own resources," not a withdrawal of a trader's own capital.

Account models

The official FAQ states: "The active Axion models currently available are 1-Step, 2-Step, Instant." Each model's path:

  • 1-Step: "One phase, straight to funded."
  • 2-Step: "The classic evaluation route" — Phase 1, Phase 2, then funded.
  • Instant: "Skip evaluation, trade funded today" — Live stage only, no evaluation.

A discrepancy to flag: the Terms page describes AxionFunded as offering "Instant, 1-Step, 2-Step and 3-Step," but the Challenge Rules matrix and FAQ only list the three models above — no "3-Step" option appears anywhere else on the site. Traders interested in a 3-Step package should confirm with support before buying.

Drawdown is stated as static across all models — the Rules page says: "Static drawdown is measured against a fixed reference level." The exact calculation basis (balance vs. equity, initial vs. current) is not spelled out in the static page text; it's instead deferred to checkout/dashboard: "The applicable percentage, calculation basis and reset timing are the values shown for your programme in checkout and the dashboard." Stated leverage is 1:100 across all models.

Pricing & promotion

PropVerdict could not extract a full price table by account size, since pricing is generated by an interactive challenge-builder tool rather than a static table — to see the exact price for a given size, go directly to axionfunded.com or checkout.

At the time of checking (Nov 26, 2026), AxionFunded was running a "65% off every challenge" promotion, applied automatically to eligible products, plus "2 free add-ons of your choice (3 on 50K and up)" and a free second account of the same size on 1-Step/2-Step 100K & 200K and Instant 50K & 100K. The promo is stated to end "30 NOV" (read as Nov 30, 2026). Account sizes referenced on the site include $10,000, $25,000, $100,000 (sample certificates) and $50,000–$200,000 (in the promo description).

AxionFunded states "100% Fee refund eligible" — the FAQ clarifies refund eligibility "depends on the programme, promotion or milestone terms attached to the order," i.e. it's not an automatic refund in every case.

Whether the split increases over time or after more payouts is not clearly stated; the site only says "Scaling path: Unlock larger opportunities through consistent execution" without numbers.

Payouts

AxionFunded states a "Bi-weekly rewards" cycle and a "24H Reward review" turnaround. Reward eligibility depends on "the active split, minimum days, verification, payment integrity and absence of rule breaches," and rewards may be held during review. Minimum withdrawal amount, specific payout methods (PayPal/Wise/crypto, etc.) and per-payout caps are not disclosed in the static content PropVerdict was able to extract.

Platforms, instruments & restrictions

The homepage does not name a specific trading platform (no mention of MT4/MT5/cTrader found in the content checked), only a general "Access simulated global markets through familiar professional platforms." Tradable instruments per the homepage ticker are Forex, Indices, Commodities, Crypto, Metals; overall leverage 1:100.

  • News trading: an example widget says "News trading enabled," but the rules matrix shows unclear placeholder marks per model/stage — PropVerdict cannot confirm with certainty which models permit news trading.
  • Weekend holding, EA/bots: similarly, the rules matrix did not render clear per-model values in this extraction; the Terms only prohibit using automation to "copy, coordinate or disguise prohibited activity."
  • Hedging: banned only in the sense of "coordinated opposite positions, group hedging, copy networks or mirroring intended to guarantee an outcome across accounts" — i.e. coordinated multi-account hedging, not single-account hedging specifically.

An objective look

AxionFunded's homepage shows "Excellent 4.8/5 · Based on 2,486 verified trader reviews" without naming the source platform (Trustpilot or otherwise) — this is self-published data PropVerdict has not independently verified. Similarly, "Powering ambitious traders in over 120 countries" and the on-site "Certificate of Achievement" records can only be checked against AxionFunded's own site, not an independent third party. PropVerdict also noted inconsistencies between marketing copy (e.g. "Up to 100% split," a "0% OFF" widget alongside a site-wide 65%-off banner) and the official Challenge Rules — traders should always verify figures at actual checkout/dashboard before deciding.

PropVerdict's take

AxionFunded has a clear three-model structure (1-Step/2-Step/Instant) with specific percentage-based daily/max loss rules, easy-to-track static drawdown, and a fairly deep Black Friday promo (65% off plus free add-ons). That said, public information is missing several important details (founding year, registered address, minimum withdrawal/payout methods, named trading platforms), and there are some inconsistencies between marketing copy and the official rules matrix. Traders should read the exact Challenge Rules table for the package they're buying, confirm actual pricing at checkout, and treat marketing figures (like "100% split" or the 4.8/5 rating) as unverified claims rather than confirmed facts.
