7 Common Mistakes That Make Traders Fail a Prop Firm Challenge

Updated: 2026-11-20 · PropVerdict

Independent reviews consistently show that pass rates and first-payout rates are not high. Most failures aren't caused by a weak trading strategy, but by breaching a firm's specific risk rules. Here are the 7 most common mistakes.

1. Not reading the Daily and Max Drawdown rules carefully

Every firm calculates drawdown differently (against balance or against the day's highest equity). Trading without knowing the exact formula used often leads to an unexpected breach. See also: What Is Drawdown in Prop Trading?

2. Over-sizing positions relative to account size

Trying to hit the profit target quickly by increasing lot size sharply raises the risk of hitting the drawdown limit after just a few losing trades.

3. Trading through high-impact news events

Many prop firms restrict or ban trading during major news releases (NFP, rate decisions, CPI, etc.). Breaching this rule can get an account closed even if the trade itself was profitable.

4. No daily risk-management plan

Trading on impulse without a pre-set maximum daily loss (set well below the firm's daily drawdown limit) makes it easy to "chase losses" by opening trade after trade, leading to a rule breach.

5. Holding trades overnight or over the weekend when not allowed

Some challenge programs ban or restrict holding positions overnight or over the weekend. Ignoring this term can get an account closed even with a sound trading strategy.

6. Chasing the profit target while ignoring consistency rules

Some firms require profit to come from multiple trading days, not just a handful of large trades (a "consistency rule"). Hitting the target mostly through one or two lucky trades can get a funded account denied or a payout withheld.

7. Buying a challenge at a firm that doesn't fit your trading style

Every firm has its own rule set, allowed instruments, and time limits. Choosing a firm without comparing these factors first (fees, drawdown, consistency rules, supported instruments) raises the risk of failing even with unchanged trading skill.

How to lower the risk of failing a challenge

Read a firm's full terms before buying, set a maximum daily loss well below the firm's limit, avoid trading around major news if you're unsure of the rule, and prioritize firms with transparent, independent reviews of pass rates and payout speed.
