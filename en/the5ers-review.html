The5ers Review: 5 Funding Programs, Drawdown & Profit Split (2026)

Updated: 2026-11-22 · Source: the5ers.com

The5ers (brand name "The 5%ers") is a prop trading firm offering 5 distinct funding programs: Bootcamp, High Stakes, Hyper Growth, Pro Growth and Instant Funding. Each program has its own number of evaluation steps, drawdown rules and profit-split ramp — this review follows The5ers' own official guide blog so traders can compare them side by side.

Important note: Like most other prop firms, trading at The5ers happens on a simulated (demo) environment — the "funded" account uses virtual capital, not real money traded directly on the market. Profit generated on the simulated account is paid out to the trader in real money once withdrawal conditions are met.

The most important difference between programs isn't just the starting profit split, but what happens when the daily loss limit is breached: Bootcamp and Hyper Growth only pause trading for the rest of the day (the account survives), while High Stakes and Pro Growth terminate the account immediately on a breach — this is a bigger factor to weigh than the profit split when choosing a program.

The5ers publicly advertises a scaling ceiling of up to $4,000,000 for Bootcamp and Hyper Growth, but according to The5ers' own guide blog, reaching that ceiling requires many consecutive profit-target cycles — it isn't capital available right after a single funded pass (see the general mechanism in What Is a Scaling Plan?). Payouts are processed on a bi-weekly cycle, with a $150 minimum withdrawal.

Consistency Rule — information is not consistent

Unlike some firms that state a single "consistency rule" threshold clearly on their terms page, The5ers does not publish one unified threshold across all programs. Independent third-party reviews disagree: some sources say the CFD program caps any single day at 50% of total profit; others describe the futures program capping 30%-40% of profit per position; some sources even state The5ers has no mandatory consistency rule at all. PropVerdict could not confirm a single official figure, so we only note the inconsistency between reviews and recommend traders read the current terms of the exact program they're buying. See the general concept in What Is a Consistency Rule in Prop Trading?

Cost to participate

The5ers does not display an easily-extractable fixed price table on its official site at the time of writing (pricing appears to render dynamically by account size, program and ongoing promotions). Some independent reviews cite starting prices of just tens of USD for small account sizes, rising with account size — PropVerdict recommends checking the live price directly on the5ers.com before deciding, since independent reviews disagree on the exact figures.

An objective look

The5ers is one of the longer-running prop firms, with a public review page on Trustpilot (PropVerdict does not cite a specific score here since it could not be reliably verified at the time of checking). The clearest advantage is the range of programs across different risk-tolerance levels (Bootcamp is gentler for beginners, Instant Funding skips evaluation entirely). Points to note: consistency-rule details and exact pricing are not consistent across sources, and some programs (High Stakes, Pro Growth) terminate the account immediately on a daily-loss breach — stricter than the "pause" mechanism on Bootcamp/Hyper Growth.

PropVerdict's take

The5ers suits traders who want a choice of funding program matched to their trading style (from safer, multi-step programs to single-step, faster-paced ones), with a publicly advertised scaling ceiling that's fairly high ($4M on two programs). That said, traders should verify the exact consistency rule, current pricing and drawdown conditions of the specific program they intend to buy directly on the official site, since independent reviews are not yet consistent on some points. There is no guarantee of passing an evaluation or reaching a payout — this remains a simulated account rewarded with real money once conditions are met, not real capital traded directly on the market.
