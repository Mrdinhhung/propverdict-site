One-Step vs Two-Step Challenge in Prop Trading: Which Should You Choose?

Updated: 2026-11-21 · PropVerdict

Before buying a challenge at any prop firm, traders should understand exactly which evaluation model the firm sells, since the number of stages directly affects the fee, the time to reach funded status, and the risk rules involved. The three most common models today are Two-Step, One-Step, and No-Evaluation (Instant) programs.

What is a Two-Step Challenge?

The traditional and most common model: the trader must clear two consecutive stages (usually called Phase 1 and Phase 2), each with its own profit target (Phase 2's target is usually lower than Phase 1's), with drawdown rules applying across both stages. The upside is a typically lower upfront fee and sometimes slightly more flexible risk rules; the downside is it takes longer to reach a funded account.

What is a One-Step Challenge?

Only a single stage needs to be cleared to receive a funded account. In exchange for reaching funded status faster, many firms charge a higher fee and/or apply tighter drawdown rules than on a Two-Step, since the firm needs to offset the risk of funding a trader who has only been tested through one stage instead of two.

No-Evaluation (Instant) programs

Some firms sell direct access to a simulated funded account without requiring any profit target to be hit first. Verified real example: InstantFunding offers an Instant Funding Lite plan — no evaluation stage, no consistency-rule requirement, with a 4% daily drawdown limit (per PropVerdict's InstantFunding review). In exchange, the upfront fee on these plans is typically relatively higher than an evaluation-based challenge of the same account size.

Quick comparison

  • Two-Step: lower fee, longer time to funded, suits traders optimizing for cost who aren't in a rush.
  • One-Step: faster time to funded, usually a higher fee and/or tighter risk rules than Two-Step, suits traders confident in their strategy who want a shorter process.
  • Instant/No-Evaluation: skips the profit-target stage entirely, the highest upfront fee of the three, suits traders who prioritize speed over cost.

How to pick the right one

New traders are usually better off starting with a Two-Step to get used to drawdown rules at a lower fee before trying faster models. Traders with a proven, consistent track record who want to save time can consider One-Step or Instant programs, as long as they've carefully run the numbers on the specific fee and risk rules of each plan — these figures vary significantly between firms, so always read the full review or the firm's official terms before signing up.
