What Is Profit Split in Prop Trading? How It's Calculated & Common Rates

Updated: 2026-11-21 · PropVerdict

Profit split is the percentage of the profit a trader keeps from a "funded" prop firm account, with the remainder going to the firm. It's one of the most important criteria when comparing prop firms, since it directly determines how much a trader actually takes home for the same trading result.

How profit split is calculated

The payout is usually as simple as: Profit generated × split rate. For example, with a 70/30 split and $1,000 in profit for the payout period, the trader receives $700 and the firm keeps $300. Some firms layer on extra rules: capping the percentage of profit payable per cycle (for example 50%), or using a tiered split instead of one flat rate.

Common profit split rates on the market

  • 70/30: a common base rate for standard funded accounts across the industry. Real example: TradeThePool applies a flat 70/30 split on funded accounts, together with a scaling program that increases account size for traders with strong performance (verified in PropVerdict's TradeThePool review).
  • 80/20 up to 90/10: many firms set their base rate in this range, or let traders pay for an add-on to raise the base rate. Some firms don't publish the base rate clearly on their official page, only the add-on rate — PropVerdict always prioritizes official figures and flags clearly when a number is only a third-party review estimate rather than confirmed data.
  • Tiered split: some firms pay a different rate depending on how much profit has been withdrawn. Real example: TradersYard pays 100% on the first $300 withdrawn, then 90% on the amount above $300 (verified in PropVerdict's TradersYard review), with a cap of 50% of profit payable per payout cycle.

What actually affects how much you take home

  • Per-cycle payout cap: some firms only let traders withdraw a portion of profit each cycle, carrying the rest to the next one.
  • Paid add-ons: raise the split rate but cost extra upfront — worth running the numbers on expected profit before buying one.
  • Scaling plans: many firms increase account size and/or split rate over time for consistently profitable traders, so the "starting" rate isn't necessarily fixed forever.
  • Payout speed and transparency: a high split rate matters less if the firm processes payouts slowly or isn't transparent about terms.

How to compare profit split across firms

When comparing, look at all of: the base split rate (and whether it's officially published or estimated), any per-cycle payout cap, whether there's a scaling plan, and actual payout speed based on independent reviews.
