What Is a Scaling Plan in Prop Trading?

Updated: Nov 23, 2026 · PropVerdict

A scaling plan is a policy some prop trading firms use to gradually increase a trader's funded account size when the trader repeatedly hits a profit target without breaching drawdown rules, cycle after cycle. It's how a firm "rewards" consistent performance instead of keeping the account size fixed for the entire relationship.

How it typically works

Details vary between firms, but the common pattern is: if a trader hits a minimum profit target (e.g. 10%) within a fixed window (e.g. every 1-4 months) and does not breach the max drawdown during that window, the funded account size increases by a fixed rate (e.g. +25% to +50%). This repeats multiple times until the firm's published capital cap is reached.

A real example

Per PropVerdict's The5ers review, two of The5ers' programs — Bootcamp and Hyper Growth — publicly state a scaling cap of up to $4,000,000. However, according to The5ers' own guide blog, reaching that cap requires sustaining multiple consecutive profit-target cycles — it is not a size you get right after your first funding, but the result of a long track record of consistent performance.

Why few traders reach the advertised cap

As PropVerdict covered in What Is Prop Trading?, pass rates for the initial challenge and first payout are already not high; reaching a scaling plan's capital cap requires repeating that performance across multiple cycles without a single drawdown breach. So an advertised cap (like "$4M") should be read as the program's theoretical maximum, not the size most real traders actually reach.

What to check before choosing a firm for its scaling plan

  • Check the specific conditions: the profit % required per cycle, the cycle length, and the % increase per step — not just the headline cap.
  • Check whether the firm publishes the exact scaling formula: some firms only state the overall cap without disclosing each step, making it hard to compare firms fairly.
  • Compare against profit split: a firm with an attractive scaling plan but a low starting profit split may be less rewarding overall than a firm offering a high split from day one, depending on your trading style.
